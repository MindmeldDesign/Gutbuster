Three floor plans

Three bets on how someone decides what to rent

Same shelves, three different front doors. Each reads left to right, the way you'd actually walk the aisle.

Aisle One · The Genre Floor

discipline first

Top nav is the department sign: Improv, Standup, Sketch. Walk into Improv and the shelves are Form: Harold, Armando, Slacker, Montage, exactly the rows already built on the homepage. Standup and Sketch would need their own shelving logic, since Form doesn't cover them.

VerdictCheapest to ship. IMPROV / STANDUP / SKETCH are already placeholder links in the NavBar, and the Form rows already exist on the homepage.
TradeoffSomeone who came for a specific theater or team has to already know which form that team runs before they can find them.

Aisle Two · The Distributor Shelf

theater first

Top nav is the theater wall: UCB, iO, Second City, Coalition, Westside, the way a store once shelved by studio. Pick a theater and its teams line up like a franchise rack. This is the closest thing Gutbuster has to browsing by "who made it" instead of "what it is."

VerdictMatches how improv nerds actually think: people follow a theater or team long before they think in terms of "Form."
TradeoffTheater and Team pages don't exist yet, and a first-time visitor with no theater loyalty has nowhere obvious to start.

Aisle Three · The New Release Wall

format first

Top nav sorts by how much time you have, not what it's about: Full Sets, Scene Clips, Promos, Bonus Features. It's the rack by the register, not the aisle you browse to learn something.

VerdictBest for someone with five minutes who wants a clip, not a night's rental. Zero new schema, all four boxes already exist as Cut Type values.
TradeoffWeak as a front door on its own: it ignores what a video is actually about, which is the first thing most people decide by.

The route we'd actually walk

The floor plan we'd build

Keep Aisle One as the front door. It's the cheapest to finish and half-built already. But bolt on the other two aisles as a filter rail instead of a second front door: exactly what a real store did when the aisles were genre, but the clerk behind the counter could still pull something by studio or off the new-release wall on request.

Standup and Sketch stay unsolved by this plan. They need their own categorization field before they can have real shelves. Until then, they can point straight at their Cut Type / Featured rows, the same shortcut the front window already uses.

The long line down the left is the front window: Featured videos skip the aisles entirely and cut straight to Watch, same as the Spotlight row does today.
